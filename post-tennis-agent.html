I built an agent to book real tennis courts in Fontainebleau

FC Freddie Chambers · 26 April 2026 · 4 min read
#agents #playwright #real-tennis #telegram
↗ live writeup · freddiecaspian.github.io/ticketing-explainer/tennis.html

Quick share on this one. Lots of chatter about agentic stuff at the moment and tbh I'm not ready to hand over my whole keychain just yet. But I do think the sweet spot right now is the slightly fiddly, repetitive flows you do over and over again — those are where the agents earn their keep.

So I built one to book me real tennis courts with pals in Fontainebleau. Finding slots is just a faff waste of time.

What it does

I type into Telegram: "Tennis with Adrien Harnay" and hit send. About two minutes later my Telegram buzzes back: Here are the top 5 slots with Adrien over the next 3 weeks…

Pick one, agent confirms, slot booked, calendar invites sent. Done.

How specific is the workflow

Deliberately narrow. Hardcoded auth, a single site (realtennisonline.com), and a handful of inputs (friend, time window, court pref). Claude isn't deciding much — it's just doing the boring fetch + filter + cross-ref against my calendar that I'd otherwise do manually.

The pieces

  1. Trigger: Telegram bot listens for natural-language requests
  2. Parser: Claude turns the message into structured params (friend, window, court)
  3. Fetcher: Playwright logs into realtennisonline.com with stored auth, scrapes available slots
  4. Filter: Cross-reference with my Google Calendar and the friend's calendar (if shared)
  5. Surface: Top 5 slots back via Telegram, formatted as buttons
  6. Confirm: User picks one, agent books it, sends calendar invites

Why I haven't gone broader

I haven't given broad security access to an open agent / paperclip thing — not ready for the mad agentic unproven workflows. But I'm sure I will soon.

The composition is the point. Tennis booking isn't Playwright or Calendar or CRM or drafting — it's all four stitched together in 90 seconds. Each capability is ordinary. The combination is what makes it feel different.

Where it'd break

  • Site changes selectors — Playwright will fail. The fix is making the agent read the page's accessibility tree first and pick selectors at runtime, but that adds 30s to every booking.
  • Auth expires — fall-back is a manual login prompt via Telegram. Not seamless but works.
  • Calendar sharing — most friends haven't actually shared their calendars with me, so cross-ref only works against my own.
