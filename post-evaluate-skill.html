The evaluate skill — a generator/evaluator loop for design

FC Freddie Chambers · 04 April 2026 · 5 min read
#multi-agent #design #skill-file #playwright
📎 evaluate.zip · drop into ~/.claude/skills/

The method is built on one insight: the AI that writes the code can't objectively judge it. It's too close to its own work. So we split the process into two roles, a generator and an evaluator, and make them iterate until the result is genuinely good.

How it works

The orchestrator (you, in your main session) writes the code, commits, and pushes. Then it spawns a fresh evaluator subagent that has zero memory of previous evaluations or the code that produced what it's looking at. The evaluator interacts with the live app through Playwright, scores it against weighted criteria, and returns structured feedback.

The orchestrator parses the scores, decides whether to refine or pivot, and loops.

brief
  → ORCHESTRATOR (writes code, commits, pushes)
        ↓
  → EVALUATOR (fresh subagent, no source code, scores via Playwright)
        ↓
  → ORCHESTRATOR (parses scores, refines or pivots)
        ↓
  loop until threshold met or max iterations

The weights are the philosophy

Default weights: design 0.35, originality 0.30, coherence 0.20, craft 0.15. Originality matters more than polish. A highly polished but generic dashboard scores lower than a rougher UI with genuine design identity.

Strong vs weak criteria

Strong criteria are specific and testable. Weak criteria are generic mush.

"Is the UX intuitive?" tells the evaluator nothing. "Can a new user complete their first booking in under 3 clicks?" gives it something to actually test.

The fresh-evaluator rule

The evaluator is spawned fresh each iteration. This is deliberate. It prevents the evaluator from anchoring on previous scores or being lenient because it saw effort. When you let evaluators carry context, they fall in love with their own framing and stop noticing new problems.

When to refine vs pivot

Refine when the composite improved 0.5+ since last iteration and the critique is about polish, not structure. Pivot when scores plateau under 6/10 or the critique is structural ("this looks like every other dashboard"). A pivot means changing the layout model, the visual metaphor, the density, or the typography approach. Swapping hex values is not a pivot.

Stopping conditions

  • Threshold met (default: all criteria ≥ 8/10)
  • Plateau (3 iterations < 0.3 improvement)
  • Branding decision blocks meaningful progress
  • Max iterations reached
