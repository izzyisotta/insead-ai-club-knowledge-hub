The voice-note pipeline behind the personal-assistant setup

FC Freddie Chambers · 02 April 2026 · 5 min read
#voice-notes #telegram #mac-mini #personal-assistant

I've built myself a personal assistant out of Obsidian (a markdown note app), Claude Code (Anthropic's AI running in the terminal), and a little Mac Mini at home that stays on 24/7. I hit a button on my phone, dictate a thought, and by the time I put the phone down, the AI has read the context, checked my calendar, drafted whatever needs drafting, and queued up an action for me to approve from Telegram.

It's less a note-taking app than a small staff of one.

What it actually feels like

I type into my phone: "Tennis with Adrien Harnay" and hit send. About two minutes later my Telegram buzzes:

Here are the top 5 slots with Adrien over the next 3 weeks…

Pick one. Slot booked. Calendar invites sent. Done in a single message exchange.

The Mac Mini is the butler

It's always on, always logged in, and it quietly handles the stuff that doesn't need me — enriching captures, re-scoring tickets, answering Telegram, sending my morning briefing podcast. My laptop does desk work. The Mac Mini does everything else.

Tasks have two priority dimensions

Most task apps have one priority field: P1, P2, P3, P4. Mine has two:

  • Priority (I set this): how important is this?
  • Urgency (machine sets this, daily): how time-sensitive is this right now?

Urgency drifts as deadlines approach. Priority is stable. The product of the two is what gets surfaced each morning.

The /draft skill

There's a skill called /draft. It does one thing: write a message that sounds like me. Before drafting anything, it reads a style guide I built from 25 real emails I've sent.

Key rules: no em dashes, British English, "v" for "very", lead with the idea not yourself, write then cut by a third, never re-explain someone's own work back to them.

The composition is the point

Tennis booking isn't Playwright or Calendar or CRM or drafting — it's all four stitched together in 90 seconds. Class prep is Playwright + Canvas + PDF parsing + ElevenLabs + vault writing. Each capability is ordinary. The combination is what makes it feel different.

What it costs me

The effect, honestly, is that I delegate about 2-3 hours of work a day. Most of that used to be friction — filing, searching, chasing, formatting, re-explaining — not the work that actually mattered. Now I get to the interesting part faster.
