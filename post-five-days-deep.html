Five days deep with Claude: a field report

TR Tom Rees · 25 April 2026 · 4 min read
#procrastination #first-impressions #confessional

I have exhausted myself from doing nothing for 5 days except play with claude. Reckon I spent about 12 hours of potential POM study time building stuff I didn't finish and so now won't use.

Worth writing up the lessons rather than pretending it didn't happen.

Day one

Got the £200 version. Spent 8 hours with claude that day. Mostly creating custom GPTs and Claude projects to do my OB project. Did the project badly because I was learning the tools instead of doing the thinking.

It is pretty addictive once you see the potential.

Day two — the productivity illusion

Realised about halfway through the day that I don't really need to ask Freddie questions anymore because I just ask Claude questions. Not sure I need to ever ask anyone questions again except Claude.

This is, I now think, the trap. Asking Claude is fast. Asking a human is slow. The fast thing is not always the better thing because the slow thing involves you actually engaging with the answer in the time it takes them to type it.

Day three — the OB lecture problem

Worried about how I am literally not listening in lectures and just playing on my laptop. How am I gonna pass these exams.

This is not a Claude problem. This is me. But Claude has made the problem worse by giving me something more interesting to do during the lecture than the lecture.

Day four — Freddie's whole system

Basically Freddie's whole system is essentially available to download online. He's reluctant to admit it but I suspect with some fine tuning it will be almost as good. The 80/20 of his setup is publicly documented; the last 20% is taste and his Mac Mini.

Spent half a day trying to replicate it. Got 30% of the way there.

Day five — what I'd actually keep

Of the 12 hours of build-time, here's what survived contact with reality:

  1. The transcript workflow. Generic enough to work across all my courses. Will use this every week.
  2. A custom GPT for case prep that's read every Bain decision-quality paper I had access to from the old job. Faster than my old method, by a lot.
  3. The career and life coach GPT that convinced me Wharton wasn't the right move. Cost me 30 minutes, saved me a year.

And the discard pile:

  • Three half-built Claude projects for individual courses
  • Two attempts at Freddie's voice-note system
  • A skill called /exam-cram that exists but does nothing useful
  • An MCP setup I gave up on after 90 minutes

The lesson

Claude makes building so cheap that you build things you'd never have started. Most of those things you also won't finish. The way out is to ask, before starting, whether the thing is generic enough to be worth keeping.

I am back in the lecture this morning. Phone is in the bag.
