§1

The argument in one slide

The Knowledge Hub is the institutional knowledge layer for INSEAD's AI ecosystem. Six stakeholder groups currently operate in parallel without crossover. The Hub is the meeting point. Build for the AI Club crowd first, then expand in four phases.
ProblemDiscoverability + persistence + hidden-use culture + cross-stream isolation (see §2)
Who it's forSix INSEAD stakeholder groups currently isolated from each other (see §3)
Strategic hypothesisA persistent, structured knowledge layer beats five chats and a mailing list, and is reframeable as institutional infrastructure rather than a club site (see §4)
Proposal4-phase rollout: research → build basic platform → wider rollout → long-run additions (see §10)
Discussion questions for AI ClubSix open questions to take into the working session (see §11)

Problem & stakeholders

§2

The problem we're solving

Four distinct pain points. Each has evidence, an existing workaround that doesn't work, and a cost of doing nothing. The Hub addresses all four; no single existing channel does. §3 then drills into how each of these pains lands on each stakeholder.
Pain point Evidence Current workaround Cost of inaction
Discoverability "I had no idea X was working on that" — heard in section chats monthly Word-of-mouth; being in the right chat at the right time Duplicated effort across cohort
Persistence Workflows shared in chats are unfindable after ~1 week Personal screenshots; DM the original sharer Knowledge dies in scrollback; new cohorts re-invent
Hidden-use culture "AI use at INSEAD feels like a dirty secret" (manifesto, verbatim) Private DMs; vague language in class Stigma persists; productive→automation jump stays psychological
Cross-stream isolation Recent admin meeting confirmed students/faculty/admin don't see each other's AI activity Ad-hoc emails; AI Club acts as broker but doesn't scale School can't see its own ground-truth; missed connections compound
Sizing (rough): if discoverability alone costs each student ~10 hours/term in duplicated effort (a conservative estimate), that's ~3,000 hours/term across the cohort. The Hub needs to recover ~10% of that to break even on the build cost alone.
§3

Stakeholders and their needs

The four problems above show up differently for each group. The Hub has to serve all six stakeholders with one product, or it ends up serving the AI Club crowd and ignoring the rest — which is the failure mode it's meant to fix.
Stakeholder Approx. size Current state Their pain What they'd contribute What they need from the Hub
AI-active students ("AI Club crowd") ~30-50 of 300 Building Claude Code workflows, vibecoded apps, skills, MCP setups; sharing in WhatsApp Their work is invisible after a week of scrollback; no audience beyond the group chat Most of the content (how-tos, skills, tools, videos) Platform to discuss & share ideas
AI-curious students ~150-200 of 300 Want to start but don't know where; intimidated by the AI-active crowd's pace No on-ramp; not in chats; don't know who to ask Audience + occasional first-attempt posts Beginner content, templates, inspiration, knowing which people to reach out to
Faculty teaching with AI ~10-20 Experimenting privately; some integration in classes No idea what students are doing with AI; can't reference student work Course-related posts, prompts they use, cautions Visibility into student practice; teaching-relevant content, sharing teaching ideas with each other - TBD?
Admin / AI strategy ~3-5 Building the school's AI strategy; need ground-truth on adoption No data on what students are doing; the AI Club is opaque to them Sponsorship, mailing list integration, possibly hosting, Faculty content about how to use AI at INSEAD - TBD how big a component this is A canonical reference for "what's happening with AI at INSEAD"
Faculty doing AI research (TBD) ~5-15 Research published in journals, invisible to students Their work doesn't reach the cohort; no student channel for dissemination Research summaries, paper recommendations, calls for collaborators A way to reach the cohort directly
Alumni (TBD) ?? Disconnected from current student practice; some AI-active in their own work No way to plug in; no ongoing tie to the school's AI conversation Possibly: senior-perspective posts, hiring signals, mentorship Decide in discovery — likely out of scope for v1
Key insight: the AI-active students are 10-15% of the school but currently absorb 100% of the AI Club's product attention. The remaining 85% (curious students + faculty + admin) are an audience the Hub could uniquely serve and currently doesn't have a channel.

Key questions

  1. Do we use this as a platform for faculty to share their teaching methodology & insights with each other?
  2. How big a role does INSEAD official content for students play here? Should it be included or is this a separate channel (LMS, INSEAD courses, etc.)
  3. What role do AI research faculty play? (/ is there anyone except Johannes)
  4. What role should alumni play?
§4

Strategic positioning to get stakeholder buy-in

"AI Club website" and "institutional knowledge layer" are the same artefact viewed through different lenses. The second lens unlocks a much bigger audience, a credible school partnership, and different platform decisions. We should build for the second.
"AI Club website" framing "Institutional knowledge layer" framing
Who it's forThe 30-50 AI-active studentsAll six stakeholder groups (~250+)
What it doesHosts workflow writeupsCaptures how the school's AI ecosystem actually operates
Success metricPosts/week from club membersCross-stream interactions; cited by school in official docs
School postureTolerated student projectEndorseable institutional asset
Funding modelPersonal funds / club budgetSchool line item by Year 2
LifespanUntil current cohort graduatesMulti-cohort; permanent infrastructure
Platform requirementsLightweight CMS, fast to shipPersistent identity, stable URL, archivable, SSO-friendly
Implication: if we accept the second framing, the platform decision shifts. A static site on GitHub answers the first framing. The second pushes us toward Next.js + Supabase with proper auth, RLS, and an audit trail (which is what the Architecture doc already lands on).

§6

Content categories

Four top-level categories, goal-oriented (what you're trying to do) rather than tool-oriented (what you're using). A faculty/research category is an open question for discovery.
Category What lives here
Workflow AutomationAutomating real INSEAD / life stuff (Canvas sync, lecture pipelines, scheduling)
Building AppsVibecoded tools, sites, side projects shipped
Agents & IterationMulti-agent setups, evaluate loops, prompting patterns, debugging tactics
General ConfigMaking Claude Code more powerful (MCP servers, hooks, plugins, settings)

Open questions

  • Should "Faculty / Teaching" be its own category if faculty post in Phase 4, or do their posts fit under the existing four?
  • Do we want a Research category for AI research happening at INSEAD (Phase 4)?
  • How does the IA hold up when content scope expands beyond the AI Club crowd?
§7

Contributor types

Each post has an author with a role label shown next to their name (in the people grid + on post bylines). Core types are derived from the stakeholder groups in §3, flattened where the platform doesn't need the distinction.

Core types

  • Student — current INSEAD MBA / MIM student. Default for most contributors. Covers both AI-active and AI-curious.
  • AI Club — student in an officer / admin role. Overlay on Student (shown as second tag, e.g. "26D · Section E06 · AI Club founder").
  • Faculty — teaching staff.
  • Admin / Staff — INSEAD non-academic staff (AI strategy, IT, comms).

Possible additions to consider

  • AI Champion — Adèle's program of designated AI-savvy faculty/staff. Could be its own role, or an overlay on Faculty/Admin.
  • Guest — external invited contributor (visiting speaker, industry friend, partner-school student). Useful if we ever do a "guest post" feature.
  • Industry partner / sponsor — only relevant if we accept sponsored content. Currently scoped out per Operations.md.
Open question: is "AI Club" a contributor type or just a profile tag on a Student? In the mockup it's used as a role label. Could go either way — flag for discovery.

Part 3

Roadmap & decisions

How we know it's working, how we sequence the build, what we're deciding by when.

§9

What would success even mean? — three positions

There's no agreed answer pre-discovery. Each position implies different metrics, different audiences, different sunset rules. Pick one (or define a hybrid) in the discovery session.
Position "Success means..." What we'd measure Sunset trigger if missed
A. Substrate works The AI Club crowd reliably writes things up 30+ contributors, 5+ posts/week <15 contributors at 6 months
B. Behaviour change Cohort thinks "look on the Hub" before asking around First-look behaviour; references in chats Zero references at 6 months
C. Institutional asset School cites the Hub as canonical AI reference At least one citation; faculty engagement Zero citations by Phase 3
Plausible sequence: A → B → C. Phase 2 hits A. Phase 3 starts hitting B. Phase 4 hits C. If A doesn't land, B and C are unreachable — so Phase 2 is the only one that can fail us out of the project.
Sunset rule (operational): if A's threshold fires by 6 months post-launch, site goes read-only with archived banner. Posts stay readable. Don't let it limp on.
§10

Phased roadmap

Research first, then build for engaged users, then expand. Each phase has a clear "ready to move" trigger; we don't progress until the prior phase has worked.
Phase What happens
1a. Research & plan Interview each stakeholder type for use cases; secure engaged faculty buy-in (Adèle / AI Champions); run discovery session
1b. Decide the practicals Pose & answer:
  • platform (e.g. own platform vs LMS)
  • domain
  • hosting
  • ownership / continuity
  • INSEAD official involvement
  • access model
  • funding
  • moderation
  • reactions & feed sort
2. Build basic platform Build version 1; engaged AI users populate it; iterate from feedback
3. Wider student rollout Market to and invite wider student body
4. Long-run additions Everything mentioned earlier: faculty posting, alumni network, partner schools, Singapore campus, possibly research category, possibly video as 5th type
Operating principle: don't try to build for everyone in v1. v1 is for the AI Club crowd. The product earns the right to expand.
