§1

The key argument

The Knowledge Hub is the institutional knowledge layer for INSEAD's AI ecosystem. Six stakeholder groups currently operate in parallel without crossover. The Hub is the meeting point. Build for the AI Club crowd first, then expand in five phases.
ProblemAwareness + discoverability + persistence + hidden-use culture + cross-stream isolation (see §2)
Who it's forSix INSEAD stakeholder groups currently isolated from each other (see §3)
Strategic hypothesisA persistent, structured knowledge layer beats five chats and a mailing list, and is reframeable as institutional infrastructure rather than a club site (see §4)
Proposal5-phase rollout: research → decide practicals → build → wider rollout → long-run additions (see §11)
Discussion questions for AI ClubSix open questions to take into the working session (see §12)

§2

The problem we're solving

Four distinct pain points. Each has evidence, an existing workaround that doesn't work, and a cost of doing nothing. The Hub addresses all four; no single existing channel does. §3 then drills into how each of these pains lands on each stakeholder.
Pain point Evidence Current workaround
Awareness of what's possible AI-curious students don't know what AI is capable of — the gap is imagination, not access. Ceiling stays low. Stumble across it on Twitter / podcasts; ask the AI Club crowd
Discoverability "I had no idea X was working on that" — heard in section chats monthly Word-of-mouth; being in the right chat at the right time
Persistence Workflows shared in chats are unfindable after ~1 week Personal screenshots; DM the original sharer
Hidden-use culture "AI use at INSEAD feels like a dirty secret" (manifesto, verbatim) Private DMs; vague language in class
Cross-stream isolation Students, faculty, and admin don't see each other's AI activity; missed connections compound Ad-hoc emails; AI Club acts as broker but doesn't scale
§3

Stakeholders and their needs

The four problems above show up differently for each group. The Hub has to serve all six stakeholders with one product, or it ends up serving the AI Club crowd and ignoring the rest — which is the failure mode it's meant to fix.
Stakeholder Current state Their pain What they'd contribute What they need from the Hub
AI-active students ("AI Club crowd")
~30-50 of 300		 Building Claude Code workflows, vibecoded apps, skills, MCP setups; sharing in WhatsApp Their work is invisible after a week of chat scrollback; ideas spread across multiple group chats and aren't cross-pollinated Most of the content (how-tos, skills, tools, videos) Platform to discuss & share ideas
AI-curious students
~150-200 of 300		 Want to start but don't know where; intimidated by the AI-active crowd's pace No on-ramp; not in chats; don't know who to ask Audience + occasional first-attempt posts Beginner content, templates, inspiration, knowing which people to reach out to
Faculty teaching with AI
~10-20		 Experimenting privately; some integration in classes No idea what students are doing with AI; can't reference student work Course-related posts, prompts they use, cautions Visibility into student practice; teaching-relevant content. Open: do faculty also want a venue to share teaching methodology with each other?
Admin / AI strategy
~3-5		 Building the school's AI strategy; need ground-truth on adoption No data on what students are doing; the AI Club is opaque to them Sponsorship, mailing list integration, possibly hosting. Open: how prominent should INSEAD-official content (faculty guidance on AI use) be on the platform? A canonical reference for "what's happening with AI at INSEAD"
Faculty doing AI research (TBD)
~5-15		 Research published in journals, invisible to students Their work doesn't reach the cohort; no student channel for dissemination Research summaries, paper recommendations, calls for collaborators A way to reach the cohort directly
Alumni (TBD)
size ??		 Disconnected from current student practice; some AI-active in their own work No way to plug in; no ongoing tie to the school's AI conversation Possibly: senior-perspective posts, hiring signals, mentorship Decide in discovery — likely out of scope for v1
Key insight: the AI-active students are 10-15% of the school but currently absorb 100% of the AI Club's product attention. The remaining 85% (curious students + faculty + admin) are an audience the Hub could uniquely serve and currently doesn't have a channel.

§4

Strategic positioning to get stakeholder buy-in

"AI Club website" and "institutional knowledge layer" are the same artefact viewed through different lenses. The second lens unlocks a much bigger audience, a credible school partnership, and different platform decisions. We should build for the second.
"AI Club website" framing "Institutional knowledge layer" framing
Who it's forThe 30-50 AI-active studentsAll six stakeholder groups (~250+)
What it doesHosts workflow writeupsCaptures how the school's AI ecosystem actually operates
Success metricPosts/week from club membersCross-stream interactions; cited by school in official docs
School postureTolerated student projectEndorseable institutional asset
Funding modelPersonal funds / club budgetSchool line item by Year 2
LifespanUntil current cohort graduatesMulti-cohort; permanent infrastructure
Platform requirementsLightweight CMS, fast to shipPersistent identity, stable URL, archivable, SSO-friendly
Implication: if we accept the second framing, the platform decision shifts. A static site on GitHub answers the first framing. The second pushes us toward Next.js + Supabase with proper auth, RLS, and an audit trail (which is what the Architecture doc already lands on).

§9

Platform options & trade-offs

Four realistic options for where the Hub lives. Each has different trade-offs across control, cost, INSEAD endorsement potential, and feature support — the right pick depends on the audience scope and ambition the AI Club agrees on.
Option Pros Cons Best for
Custom-built
Next.js + Supabase, own domain		 Full control over UX, branding, data; supports all features in §8; persistent identity; portable ~6-8 weeks build effort; ongoing maintenance; founder-handover risk; need to provision auth + DB Long-term institutional infrastructure
LMS integration
inside INSEAD's existing LMS		 Already has INSEAD SSO; school-blessed by default; lives across cohorts; zero hosting cost Constrained UX; admin gatekeeping; may not support all content types; brand-locked; harder to surface to non-students Speed-to-launch with school endorsement
GitHub Pages
static site, public repo		 Free hosting; transparent (open source); fast to ship; familiar to AI Club crowd No native auth, comments, saves, or reactions; not INSEAD-branded; public-only by default; intimidating to non-technical users — alienates the AI-curious audience the Hub is meant to onboard Quick proof of concept
SaaS
Notion / Substack / similar		 Zero build effort; templated; immediate launch; low maintenance Limited UX control; data lock-in; doesn't feel purpose-built; weak access controls Concept testing before committing to a real build
Decision rule: the platform should match the audience scope and ambition the AI Club agrees on (see §12 Q3 for the discussion). Picking a platform without that anchor is how products end up serving the wrong audience.

Roadmap & decisions

§10

What would success even mean? — three positions

There's no agreed answer pre-discovery. Each position implies different metrics, different audiences, different sunset rules. Pick one (or define a hybrid) in the discovery session.
Position "Success means..." What we'd measure Sunset trigger if missed
A. Substrate works The AI Club crowd reliably writes things up 30+ contributors, 5+ posts/week <15 contributors at 6 months
B. Behaviour change Cohort thinks "look on the Hub" before asking around First-look behaviour; references in chats Zero references at 6 months
C. Institutional asset School cites the Hub as canonical AI reference At least one citation; faculty engagement Zero citations by Phase 3
Plausible sequence: A → B → C. Phase 2 hits A. Phase 3 starts hitting B. Phase 4 hits C. If A doesn't land, B and C are unreachable — so Phase 2 is the only one that can fail us out of the project.
Sunset rule (operational): if A's threshold fires by 6 months post-launch, site goes read-only with archived banner. Posts stay readable. Don't let it limp on.
§11

Phased roadmap

Research first, then build for engaged users, then expand. Each phase has a clear "ready to move" trigger; we don't progress until the prior phase has worked.
1
Research & plan
Interview each stakeholder type for use cases; secure engaged faculty buy-in (Adèle / AI Champions); run discovery session.
2
Decide the practicals
Pose & answer:
  • platform (e.g. own platform vs LMS)
  • domain
  • hosting
  • ownership / continuity
  • INSEAD official involvement
  • access model
  • funding
  • moderation
  • reactions & feed sort
3
Build basic platform
Build version 1; engaged AI users populate it; iterate from feedback.
4
Wider student rollout
Market to and invite wider student body.
5
Long-run additions
Everything mentioned earlier: faculty posting, alumni network, partner schools, Singapore campus, possibly research category, possibly video as 5th type.
Operating principle: don't try to build for everyone in v1. v1 is for the AI Club crowd. The product earns the right to expand.
